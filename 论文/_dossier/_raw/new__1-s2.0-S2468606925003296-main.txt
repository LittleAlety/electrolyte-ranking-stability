ABSTRACT
Determining the redox potential of electrolytes is crucial for exploring the electrochemical stability window in 
rechargeable batteries. However, the commonly used thermodynamic cycle method for calculating redox po -
tentials does not account for the complex lithium-ion (Li
+
) coordination environment around redox-active sites. 
We conducted a systematic investigation of a 140 Li
+
-coordinated electrolytes library and discovered that the 
elevation in redox potential caused by Li
+
is attributable to a combined effect of electrostatic and ion-dipole 
interactions. These two types of factors, however, can perform converse effects on the change of oxidation 
and reduction potentials, respectively. Among the various machine learning (ML) models examined, the extreme 
gradient boosting (XGB) algorithm demonstrates the best predictive accuracy for the redox potential of elec -
trolytes, both with and without Li
+
coordination. The integration of high-throughput calculation and ML creates 
a powerful platform for rapidly predicting electrolyte redox potentials, essential for accurately assessing practical 
electrochemical performance. Moreover, the current work highlights the role of electronic features in deter -
mining oxidation potential and structural features in influencing reduction potential, providing valuable insights 
for the design and application of new electrolytes.
1. Introduction
The redox potential is a nuanced indicator of a redox-active species ’ 
propensity for electron addition or removal, fundamentally shaped by 
the intricate interplay of its electronic and chemical architecture [ 1 – 3 ]. 
Accurately predicting redox potential of electrolytes enables the rational 
design of wide electrochemical windows (ECW), aligning with emerging 
high-voltage cathodes to maximize energy density in rechargeable 
lithium-ion batteries (LiBs) and drive innovation in energy storage 
technologies [ 4 , 5 ]. Experimental measurements using cyclic voltam -
metry often encounter challenges related to reversible processes, 
complicating the discovery of new compounds and the screening of 
suitable electrolyte candidates with appropriate ECW for rechargeable 
batteries [ 6 ]. The quantum mechanical (QM) Born-Haber thermody -
namic cycle method allows the determination of redox potential by 
evaluating the gas phase ionization/affinities energies and the solvation 
energy [ 7 – 9 ]. Notably, the trustworthy results are not easy to obtain, 
which highly depends on the reliable description of the redox process 
and the precise quantum-chemical approaches used, including the se -
lection of solvation model, functional, and other parameters specific to 
each class of compounds [ 8 – 11 ]. In particular, the electrodes of LiBs are 
surrounded by a lithium-rich environment, allowing the electrolyte to 
undergo oxidation/reduction reaction in combination with lithium ions.

ture, and polarization, which in turn alter values of reduction/oxidation 
potentials [ 10 , 12 , 13 ]. However, the most commonly used thermody -
namic cycle patterns often overlook the impact of the lithium cation, 
highlighting the need to compare redox behavior across different elec -
trolyte environments. On the other hand, larger-scale QM calculations 
for hundreds of molecules are typically constrained by their resource 
intensity, thus limiting the feasibility of extensive studies. To enhance 
prediction accuracy and reduce computational burden while uncovering 
underlying relationships in date related to various properties and be -
haviors, a cost-effective alternative for reliable estimation of redox po -
tential is essential.
Recently, data-driven machine learning (ML) approaches have 
emerged as powerful tools for quantitatively analyzing structure- 
property relationships (QSPR) from historical data, thereby acceler -
ating the discovery of new materials and facilitating advancements in 
material design and optimization [ 14 – 22 ]. The ML algorithm has a 
black-box nature that can uncover linear correlations between ener -
gy/structural characteristics and the redox potential of electroactive 
molecules. For example, Assary et al. used a Gaussian Process Regression 
(GPR) technique based on multi-objective Bayesian optimization (MBO) 
to accelerate the search for new candidates with optimal target values 
(reduction potential, solvation energy and absorption wavelength) [ 18 ]. 
The optimized MBO algorithm still shows high generalizability and ef -
ficiency for identifying ideal molecules over larger data set [ 19 ]. Tabor 
et al. trained a GPR model using experimentally measured reduction 
potentials from OROP datasets, augmented with eight additional com -
pounds [ 21 ]. This model utilized molecular fingerprints and 3D de -
scriptors, demonstrating greater accuracy than a simple linear model. 
Given the accelerated statistics of redox potential can be achieved by 
sophisticated ML algorithm with fine-tuning hyperparameters, the pre -
diction accuracy of the ML models typically depends on the diversity and 
the quantity of data used for training the models. Kubo et al. utilized 
simple structural descriptors to distinguish 149 electrolyte additives. 
Both applied Gaussian kernel ridge regression (GKRR) and gradient 
boosting regression (GBR) models exhibited well prediction for oxida -
tion potentials, while the models performed poorly in predicting 
reduction potentials, likely due to bond cleavage occurring in the 
reduced state [ 14 ]. Vanka et al. extracted features of phenazine de -
rivatives using the RDKit library and trained the database with four 
different ML algorithms [ 22 ]. It was observed that the redox potential is 
highly related to the “ PEOB_VSA1 ” descriptor, which reflects the delo -
calization of partial charge for phenazine derivatives. While numerous 
studies have been developed for the rapid prediction of thousands, or 
even millions, of redox potentials for electrolyte molecules, there are no 
relevant ML algorithms specifically focusing on redox properties 
considering the coordination of Li
+
.
In this study, we utilized ML-based predictive approaches to advance 
the exploration of lithium-coordinated redox properties for a total of 140 
electrolyte molecules. Two thermodynamic cycle patterns (with and 
without Li
+
) were considered here to obtain the input database, sepa -
rately, leveraging high-throughput density functional theory (DFT) 
calculations. We identified the molecular geometry using SMILES sub -
strings and extracted corresponding electronic/structural descriptors 
from the database. The prediction accuracy of the models was enhanced 
through hyperparameter tuning and feature selection. Then, the SHap -
ley Additive Explanations (SHAP) were performed to provide an indi -
vidual interpretation of each feature to the total predictive model. Our 
research expanded the analysis of Li
+
-binding redox behavior across 
various electrolyte components, providing a predictive framework and 
screening guidelines for the rational design of optimized electrolytes.
2. Method
The fundamental process for the ML paradigm aimed at efficient 
prediction of electrolyte redox properties is illustrated in Fig. 1 . This 
includes data generation, feature selection, model training, and evalu -
ation [ 23 , 24 ]. The initial electrolyte components were extracted from 
previously reported theoretical/experimental literatures [ 25 , 26 ]. To 
ensure model transferability, we focus on molecular derivatives with 
diverse backbone structures, enabling the model to learn more gener -
alized features and adopt varied structures. Additionally, the distribu -
tion of the investigated electrolyte molecules, categorized by chemical 
type and functional group, is presented in Fig. S1 and Fig. S2 , respec -
tively, demonstrating the database ’ s broad coverage. The following data 
points can be derived through DFT calculations. Feature selection is the 
most crucial step to construct an optimal ML model, as it can remove 
irrelevant and redundant descriptors, thereby shortening training time, 
reducing overfitting or underfitting, and improving model interpret -
ability. The collected raw data are often unrefined and require further 
preprocessing, including the removal of null values and data standard -
ization, etc. After that, six ML algorithms were applied for model 
training, with evaluation by coefficient of determination (R
2
) and mean 
absolute error (MAE). The SHAP analysis was applied to interpret the 
well-trained models, providing a deeper understanding of how the fea -
tures influence the target variables.
